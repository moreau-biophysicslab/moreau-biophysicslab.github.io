Two complementary themes connect our work: bacterial biofilms and the mechanics of red blood cells.

01 / BACTERIAL BIOFILMS

How do cells build
a living material?

Collective organization. Material properties.

Biofilms are communities of bacteria embedded in an extracellular matrix. They offer a setting in which to explore how the behaviour of individual cells relates to the structure and physical properties of a community.

Our research interests include biofilm architecture, mechanical behaviour and the relationship between cells and their surrounding matrix. Alexis Moreau’s publications bring together questions from microbiology, soft matter physics and quantitative biology.

02 / RED BLOOD CELLS

How do cells adapt
to confined flow?

Deformation. Flow. Filtration.

Red blood cells travel through narrow passages and undergo substantial changes in shape. Their mechanics and interactions influence how they move and organize in confined environments.

Alexis Moreau’s work examines collective organization in microfluidic flows and the physical mechanisms of splenic filtration, bringing together experimental observations and modelling.
